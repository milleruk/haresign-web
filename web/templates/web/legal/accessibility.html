Our commitment

Haresign works with people running general practice — a setting where access needs are taken seriously — and we want this website to be usable by as many people as possible, including those using assistive technology.

This statement covers the public Haresign website. It says what we have built in, and it is honest about what has not yet been checked.

The standard we work to

This site is designed to meet the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.2 at level AA.

We do not claim formal compliance. The site has not been independently audited, and “designed to meet” is a description of how it was built, not a verified conformance claim.

What we have built in

  • Semantic structure. Real landmarks — header, main, footer — one <h1> per page, and headings that descend in order, so the page can be navigated by structure rather than by sight.
  • Skip link. The first thing a keyboard user reaches is a link straight to the main content, so no visit begins by tabbing through the navigation.
  • Keyboard navigation. Every interactive element is reachable and operable by keyboard. The mobile menu closes on Escape and returns focus to the button that opened it.
  • Visible focus. A clear focus ring on every focusable element, with a high-contrast variant for Windows High Contrast Mode.
  • Colour contrast. Text and interface colours are checked against WCAG thresholds rather than chosen by eye. Body text is 7.6:1 on white; the primary button is 5.0:1; the brand colours were also checked for separation under red-green colour-vision deficiency.
  • Colour is never the only cue. Anything distinguished by colour is also labelled in words.
  • Reduced motion. Movement on the site is decorative, and it is removed entirely when your system asks for reduced motion.
  • Responsive layout, tested in a browser. Every page is checked automatically at seven widths from 320 to 1440 pixels: nothing scrolls sideways, no image escapes its column, and wide tables scroll inside their own area rather than breaking the page.
  • Touch targets. Controls are at least 24 × 24 pixels, and the main actions considerably larger. This is checked automatically at every mobile width rather than by eye.
  • Meaningful links. Link text describes its destination, so it makes sense read out of context. In body copy links are underlined, not signalled by colour alone.
  • Images. Decorative graphics are hidden from assistive technology; images that carry meaning are given alternative text.
  • No hidden dependencies. The site loads no external fonts, scripts or trackers, so it behaves the same on a restricted network.

Known limitations

We would rather tell you these than let you discover them.

  • No independent audit. The site has not been assessed by an external accessibility auditor.
  • No screen-reader or user testing yet. Accessibility has been built in, checked by code review, and tested in a browser at seven screen sizes — but the site has not been tested with a screen reader or with disabled users. That is planned before it replaces the current haresign.net.
  • Article header images are treated as decorative. Every article carries a header image whose visible text is the article's own headline — which is already on the page, right beside it. Describing it would make a screen reader read the same words twice, so each is marked decorative and skipped. That is a decision, not an omission. Where such an image ever carries something the headline does not, it gets a description; we are reviewing them for that.
  • A few older articles skip a heading level. Four places in the archive jump from a second-level heading to a fourth. It makes those pages slightly harder to navigate by structure. They are being corrected as editorial work rather than by a script rewriting published articles.
  • Data tables and charts. Some analysis includes wide tables and chart images. Wide tables scroll within their own area rather than breaking the page, but a chart supplied as an image may not yet have a full text equivalent.
  • Printable checklists in some articles. A few articles include tick-box checklists meant to be printed. The boxes can be focused and ticked on screen, but nothing is saved.
  • This site is in beta. It is under active development and parts of it will change.

Reporting a problem

If something on this site is difficult or impossible to use, please tell us — it is the fastest way for us to fix it.

Email {{ legal.contact_email }}. It helps if you can say what page you were on, what you were trying to do, and what browser or assistive technology you were using. We will acknowledge your message and tell you what we intend to do about it.

If you need something on this site in another format, ask us and we will do what we reasonably can.

Changes to this statement

We will update this statement as the site changes and as testing is completed. The “last reviewed” date at the top of the page reflects the most recent change.
